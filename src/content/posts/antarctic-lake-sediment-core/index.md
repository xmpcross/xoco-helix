The core arrives at the surface as a narrow cylinder of dark mud. Its value lies in the order of its layers.

## Protecting the sequence

Field teams photograph, label, cool, and stabilize each section before transport. A disturbed layer can blur the boundary between seasons or move younger material into an older interval.

Back in the laboratory, researchers compare grain size, organic molecules, pollen, pigments, and microscopic remains. Each proxy responds to a different part of the environment.

## Building an age model

Depth is not the same as time. Sediment accumulates unevenly, and occasional floods or slumps can deposit material rapidly.

Radiometric dates and identifiable chemical markers anchor the sequence. Statistical age models then express a range of plausible dates for every depth rather than a single perfect timeline.

## A complementary archive

Ice cores excel at preserving atmospheric gases and snowfall chemistry. Lake sediments can retain ecological and erosion signals that extend the local picture.
