Fast radio bursts are brief, bright, and inconveniently distant. Those same qualities make them useful probes of material that emits little light of its own.

## A delay with information inside it

As a radio pulse travels through ionized gas, lower frequencies are delayed more than higher ones. Measuring that spread gives astronomers an estimate of the electrons encountered along the route.

With a reliable distance to the burst's host galaxy, the delay can be compared with models of gas inside the Milky Way, the host, and the space between galaxies.

## The foreground problem

No single burst provides a clean cosmic inventory. Dense plasma around the source can mimic part of the intergalactic signal, while the Milky Way contribution varies across the sky.

The method becomes more powerful as the catalog grows. Researchers can look for population-level patterns and identify sight lines where another galaxy or halo adds a measurable contribution.

## Precision through repetition

The next step is less about finding one spectacular flash than about building a well-characterized sample with secure host identifications and transparent selection effects.
