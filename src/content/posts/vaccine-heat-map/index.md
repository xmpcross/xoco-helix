Extreme heat behaves differently from a virus, but public-health teams increasingly map it with similar discipline. Who is exposed? Who is vulnerable? Where can intervention happen before harm arrives?

## A Forecast Is Not a Care Plan

Temperature forecasts identify hazard. Health maps identify people and places likely to be harmed by that hazard.

## Why the Map Needs More Than Weather

Air conditioning access, housing quality, chronic illness, medications, tree cover, and job type can all change risk. A neighborhood with moderate temperatures can still be dangerous if residents cannot cool down.

## The Hard Part

Targeting help without stigmatizing communities requires careful privacy rules and local partnerships.
