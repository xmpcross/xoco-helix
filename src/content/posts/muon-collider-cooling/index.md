Muon colliders have a strange appeal: they promise electron-like cleanliness at energies that would otherwise demand enormous machines. The catch is built into the particle. Muons decay quickly.

## The Cooling Problem

Particles in a beam need to be squeezed into a tight phase-space volume before collision. Muons do not wait politely while engineers do this. A collider concept therefore becomes a timing problem as much as an energy problem.

## Why Not Just Build Bigger?

Circular electron machines lose energy rapidly through synchrotron radiation at high energies. Protons can go higher, but their collisions are messier because protons are composite. Muons sit in the tempting middle.

> The muon collider is a machine proposal where the stopwatch is part of the physics.

## What Would Count as Progress

Demonstrations of cooling, targetry, detector shielding, and cost realism matter more than another beautiful conceptual diagram.
