A river plume is not a line on a map. It is a moving body of water with different temperature, color, chemistry, and density.

## Seeing the pulse

Satellite imagery can track suspended sediment and chlorophyll, while models estimate how wind and currents move the plume after storms.

## Why timing matters

Communities downstream care about where the plume carries nutrients, pollutants, and heat. A forecast can help target sampling boats and protect sensitive sites.

## The field check

Water samples still anchor the remote signal. Without them, color can be mistaken for chemistry and a sharp boundary can hide vertical structure.
