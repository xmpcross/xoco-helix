A nursery is often drawn as a boundary on a map. Tagged juveniles are showing that the lived habitat can look more like a network.

## Following repeated returns

Acoustic tags transmit short identification codes. Fixed receivers record a fish when it passes nearby, allowing researchers to reconstruct recurring routes without continuously following the animal.

The record is incomplete by design. Silence can mean that a shark left the area, passed between receivers, or remained just beyond detection range.

## The connective habitat

Across seasons, juveniles used protected shallows, tidal channels, and nearby coastal water. The movement pattern changed with temperature and likely prey conditions.

That matters for management because a protected nursery may depend on corridors and feeding areas outside its formal boundary.

## Reading the gaps

Researchers test alternative movement models and compare receiver coverage before describing a route as preferred. The strongest findings come from patterns repeated by many individuals over multiple years.
