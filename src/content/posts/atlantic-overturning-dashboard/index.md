The Atlantic Meridional Overturning Circulation is often described as a conveyor belt. The image is useful until it is too useful. A belt feels singular. The real system is a turbulent, density-driven, wind-influenced circulation that researchers infer through instruments, proxies, and models.

## Why One Number Is Not Enough

An array can tell scientists what is happening along one section. It cannot, by itself, describe every process across the basin or every century-scale trend.

## The Useful Dashboard

A better reader model is a dashboard: transport estimates, water temperature, salinity, sea level, deep-water formation, model ensembles, and paleoclimate context. Each signal has a different temporal grain.

> The serious question is not whether one chart looks alarming. It is whether independent lines of evidence are becoming harder to reconcile with stability.

## Reading Risk Without Theater

The evidence for weakening deserves attention. So does the uncertainty. Treating the system like a countdown clock may create urgency, but it can also make the science look more settled than it is.

[^1]: Climate-system thresholds are not calendar appointments. They are risk distributions shaped by emissions, feedbacks, and internal variability.
