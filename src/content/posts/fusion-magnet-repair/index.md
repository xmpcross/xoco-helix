A large superconducting magnet is not a component that technicians can lift out, repair on a bench, and reinstall by the end of a shift.

## Access is an engineering problem

Cooling lines, supports, shielding, diagnostics, and neighboring magnets occupy the same constrained volume. A repair sequence must account for every tool path and temporary load.

Engineers build full-scale mock-ups to practice movements, test cameras, and measure whether a remote manipulator can apply force without damaging adjacent hardware.

## Proving the repair

The tool must also collect evidence. Electrical tests, images, leak checks, and dimensional measurements are planned into the sequence so that the team can distinguish a successful intervention from a hidden defect.

## Designing for the next machine

Maintenance rehearsals feed back into future facilities. A few centimeters of additional clearance or a better-positioned connector can change the feasibility of a repair years later.
